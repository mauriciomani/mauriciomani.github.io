Acidity, tannins, sugar, alcohol and body... How to really understand what is going on?

I was in a free wine tasting the other day were we tried 3 Louis Latour wines, Ardeche chardonnay, Pouilly Fuisse, and Beaujolais-Villages, all 2024 vintages. 
To be honest most of the folks we were there were completely out of the wine world and probably just there becuase of the free wine. 
However, some participants were trying to play the connoisseur, with words like fresh, vibrant, clean, easy-to-drink.
The cool thing was that we could compare Pouilly Fuisse and Ardeche, find the differences and the one we like the most.
Nothing to fancy but it made me doubt my senses, of course every one will have a different perceptions and appreciation and that is ok.
But, I think you can like any wine you want, but you need to understand why you like the wine.
For example, I felt Ardeche alcoholic due to the lack of body, how dry it was and how low acidity the wine had, 
but there were some folks describing it as fresh and vibrant. 
Then not understanding why Pouilly Fuisse was more fresh, with a full body, ending up in an elegant wine, 
making the alcohol very subtle compared to the Ardeche; basically I blame how dry Ardeche was. 
Even though is branded as a daily wine is not the type of wine that I would like to drink. 
So it made me thought about the importance of understanding wine components, so I setup an experiment. For the experiment, I needed:
* Lactic acid
* Tartaric acid
* Citric acid
* Malic acid
* Tanins
* Sugar

You can get all of them online, you can just try them like candies, but lets see what happens when we add it to wines, the idea is to understand how sugar changes, wine, how acidity changes wine and how tannins change wine. So we need to find a dry wine with low acidity and low presence of tannins. There is a varietal, that usually has dry, low acidic wines: Viognier, so I picked Don Luis Viognier from L.A. Cetto, is a not very expensive white wine. So, with multiple glasses we can see the difference between malic, citric, tartaric and lactic acid.

Malic and citric acids are very similar and the type of acids you will find in white wines, my feeling was they hit specific places in your mouth and they stayed there. Then if you try tartaric acid, the acid distributes throughout you whole mouth, without attacking specific places, if you finally have the lactic acid, is the one that distributes in your mouth the most. So those 2 last acids do not hit on your mouth making the wine feels very acidic, instead they distribute the acid in all your mouth.

In anothger glass you can try adding sugar, this will help you understand if you prefer sweet or dry wines, remember to always have a test glass that is just the pure Viognier wine. The pure Viognier wine feels like drinking reduced grape alcohol, the alcohol feels a lot, now, if you add sugar, you no longer feel the alcohol warm hit.
